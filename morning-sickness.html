Morning Sickness: Causes, Remedies, and When It Ends

Everything you need to know about morning sickness during pregnancy — causes, proven remedies, when it starts and ends, and when to see a doctor.

Morning sickness affects up to 80% of pregnant women, making it one of the most common early pregnancy symptoms. Despite its name, nausea and vomiting can strike at any time of day. The good news? It usually resolves by the second trimester and there are many effective remedies.

What Causes Morning Sickness?

Morning sickness is primarily caused by the rapid rise in pregnancy hormones, especially hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) and estrogen. Other contributing factors include:

  • Hormonal changes — Rising hCG and estrogen levels
  • Enhanced sense of smell — Certain odors can trigger nausea
  • Stress and fatigue — Can worsen symptoms
  • Empty stomach — Can make nausea worse

When Does Morning Sickness Start and End?

For most women, morning sickness begins around week 6 of pregnancy and peaks around weeks 8-10. Most women find relief by weeks 12-16 (the start of the second trimester). However, some women experience it throughout their entire pregnancy.

10 Proven Remedies for Morning Sickness

1. Eat Small, Frequent Meals

An empty stomach can make nausea worse. Try eating 5-6 small meals throughout the day instead of 3 large ones. Keep crackers by your bedside for a quick bite before getting up.

2. Stay Hydrated

Sip water, ginger tea, or clear broth throughout the day. If plain water triggers nausea, try adding lemon slices or drinking coconut water.

3. Ginger

Ginger is a well-known natural remedy for nausea. Try ginger tea, ginger candies, ginger ale (real ginger), or ginger supplements.

4. Avoid Trigger Foods

Identify and avoid foods that trigger your nausea. Common culprits include spicy, greasy, or strongly flavored foods.

5. Vitamin B6

Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) has been shown to reduce nausea in pregnancy. Talk to your provider about the right dosage (typically 10-25mg, 3 times daily).

6. Get Fresh Air

Open windows, take walks, or sit outside. Fresh air and gentle movement can help reduce nausea.

7. Acupressure Wristbands

Sea-Bands and similar acupressure wristbands stimulate the P6 pressure point on your wrist, which can help relieve nausea.

8. Rest

Fatigue can worsen nausea. Make sure you're getting enough sleep and take naps when needed.

9. Peppermint

Peppermint tea or peppermint candies can help soothe your stomach. Some women find the scent of peppermint essential oil helpful.

10. Avoid Strong Smells

If certain odors trigger your nausea, try to avoid them. Cook foods that don't produce strong smells, and ask your partner to handle cooking when possible.

When to See a Doctor

While morning sickness is normal, contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

  • Unable to keep any food or fluids down for 24+ hours
  • Weight loss of more than 5 pounds
  • Dark urine or infrequent urination (signs of dehydration)
  • Blood in your vomit
  • Rapid heartbeat
  • Dizziness or fainting

These could be signs of hyperemesis gravidarum, a severe form of morning sickness that requires medical treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Studies suggest that women who experience morning sickness may have a lower risk of miscarriage. However, not having morning sickness doesn't mean your pregnancy is unhealthy — every pregnancy is different.

Mild to moderate morning sickness generally doesn't harm your baby. However, severe, untreated nausea (hyperemesis gravidarum) can lead to dehydration and weight loss, which may affect your baby's growth.

Some anti-nausea medications are considered safe during pregnancy, but always consult your healthcare provider before taking any medication. Never take medication without your provider's approval.
