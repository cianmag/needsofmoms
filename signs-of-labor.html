Signs of Labor: When to Go to the Hospital

Know the signs of labor and when to head to the hospital. Learn the difference between true and false labor, and what to expect during early labor.

As your due date approaches, you may find yourself analyzing every twinge and contraction. Knowing the signs of labor can help you feel more confident about when it's time to head to the hospital.

Early Signs of Labor

1. "Lightening" (Baby Dropping)

Weeks or hours before labor, your baby may descend lower into your pelvis. This is called "lightening" or "dropping." You may notice it's easier to breathe but you need to urinate more frequently.

2. Loss of Mucus Plug

The mucus plug seals your cervix during pregnancy. When your cervix begins to soften and dilate, you may lose this plug — it looks like thick, possibly blood-tinged mucus. This can happen days or even weeks before labor.

3. Bloody Show

A pink or blood-tinged discharge is called "bloody show" and is a sign that your cervix is beginning to dilate. This usually means labor is hours to a few days away.

4. Water Breaking

The rupture of the amniotic sac ("water breaking") happens before labor begins for about 15% of women. It can feel like a gush or a slow trickle. Note the time and call your provider. Only about 15% of women experience this before contractions start.

5. Regular Contractions

True labor contractions come at regular intervals, get progressively closer together, and increase in intensity. Time your contractions from the start of one to the start of the next.

True Labor vs. False Labor (Braxton Hicks)

How to tell the difference:

False labor (Braxton Hicks): Irregular, don't get closer together, stop with rest or position change, felt mostly in front, uncomfortable but not painful.

True labor: Regular intervals, get closer and stronger over time, continue regardless of activity, start in back and wrap around to front, increasingly painful.

When to Go to the Hospital

The general rule is the 5-1-1 rule: head to the hospital when contractions are every 5 minutes, lasting 1 minute each, for at least 1 hour. However, call your provider immediately if:

  • Your water breaks (especially if the fluid is green or brown)
  • You have heavy vaginal bleeding
  • You experience severe, constant abdominal pain
  • You notice decreased fetal movement
  • You have a severe headache with vision changes
  • You've had a previous rapid labor

What Happens at the Hospital

When you arrive, you'll be assessed in triage. The nurse will check your cervix, monitor your contractions, and check the baby's heart rate. If you're in active labor (typically 6cm dilation), you'll be admitted.
