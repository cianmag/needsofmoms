Birth Plan: Your Complete Guide and Template

Create your perfect birth plan. Learn what to include, how to communicate your preferences, and get a flexible template for your birth experience.

A birth plan is a document that communicates your preferences for labor, delivery, and postpartum care to your healthcare team. While birth doesn't always go as planned, having a birth plan helps you feel informed, empowered, and prepared.

What Is a Birth Plan?

A birth plan is not a rigid script — it's a flexible guide that expresses your wishes and priorities. Think of it as a starting point for conversations with your healthcare provider about what matters most to you.

What to Include in Your Birth Plan

Labor Preferences

  • Who do you want in the room during labor?
  • Do you want the freedom to move, walk, or use a birthing ball?
  • What pain management do you prefer? (natural, epidural, nitrous oxide, or undecided)
  • Do you want music, dimmed lights, or aromatherapy?
  • Do you want to eat and drink during labor?

Delivery Preferences

  • Do you want to push in a specific position?
  • Would you like to see the birth with a mirror?
  • Who will cut the cord? Do you want delayed cord clamping?
  • Do you want immediate skin-to-skin contact?
  • Do you have preferences about episiotomy?

Postpartum Preferences

  • Do you plan to breastfeed or bottle-feed?
  • Do you want the baby to room-in with you?
  • When do you want visitors?
  • Do you want the baby bathed immediately or delayed?
  • Do you want photos/video of the birth?

Special Circumstances

  • C-section preferences (if needed)
  • Multiple birth plans (twins, triplets)
  • VBAC (vaginal birth after cesarean) preferences
  • Religious or cultural considerations
  • Placenta preferences

Tips for Creating Your Birth Plan

✨ Keep it simple: Your birth plan should be 1-2 pages. Use bullet points and clear language. Your healthcare team will appreciate a concise, easy-to-read document.

  • Discuss it early: Share your birth plan with your provider during your third trimester
  • Be flexible: Include your preferences but acknowledge that medical situations may require changes
  • Prioritize: Identify your top 3-5 must-haves
  • Prepare for alternatives: Think about your preferences for different scenarios
  • Make copies: Bring copies for your provider, nurse, and birth partner
