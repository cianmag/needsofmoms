Third Trimester

Third Trimester Weeks 28-42

The home stretch! Your baby is rapidly gaining weight, practicing for life outside the womb, and getting into position for birth. Time to finalize your birth plan and prepare for the big day.

← Second Trimester All Weeks

Overview of the Third Trimester

The third trimester is the final stretch of pregnancy. Over these 15 weeks (28-42), your baby nearly doubles in weight, develops functioning lungs, and gets into the head-down position for birth. For mom, the growing belly brings new physical challenges as you prepare for labor and delivery.

This trimester is filled with anticipation, frequent doctor visits, and important preparations. You'll pack your hospital bag, finalize your birth plan, and start watching for signs of labor. The countdown to meeting your baby begins!

Key Milestones

Week 28-31

Baby's eyes can open and close. Rapid brain development. Responds to light, sound, and touch. Practice breathing continues.

Week 32-35

Baby gains weight rapidly. Bones harden (except skull). Toenails and fingernails grow. May settle into head-down position.

Week 36-37

Early term. Baby is considered viable for delivery. Organs are nearly mature. Baby "drops" into pelvis (lightening).

Week 38-42

Full term at 39 weeks. Baby is fully developed and ready for birth. Watch for labor signs. Most babies arrive within this window.

Common Third Trimester Symptoms

The final weeks bring their own set of symptoms as your body prepares for labor:

  • Shortness of breath — Your uterus pushes up against your diaphragm
  • Heartburn — Hormones relax the valve between stomach and esophagus
  • Frequent urination — Baby's head presses on your bladder
  • Sleep difficulties — Finding a comfortable position becomes harder
  • Swelling (edema) — Especially in feet, ankles, and hands
  • Braxton Hicks contractions — More frequent practice contractions
  • Back pain — Increased weight and shifted center of gravity
  • Pelvic pressure — Baby drops and presses down
  • Varicose veins & hemorrhoids — Increased blood volume and pressure
  • Leaking breasts — Colostrum may begin to leak
  • Anxiety about labor — Very normal; consider a birth class

Tips & Advice for the Third Trimester

  1. Attend all prenatal appointments — Now every 2 weeks, then weekly from week 36
  2. Pack your hospital bag — By week 36, have it ready by the door
  3. Finalize your birth plan — Discuss preferences with your provider
  4. Count baby kicks — Track daily movements; contact provider if decreased
  5. Take a childbirth class — Learn about labor stages, breathing, and pain options
  6. Prepare the nursery — Set up crib, wash baby clothes, install car seat
  7. Rest when you can — Sleep on your left side with pillows for support
  8. Stay hydrated & eat well — Small frequent meals help with heartburn
  9. Know the signs of labor — Regular contractions, water breaking, bloody show
  10. Prepare for breastfeeding — Consider taking a breastfeeding class
  11. Arrange postpartum support — Line up help for after baby arrives
  12. Practice relaxation techniques — Deep breathing, meditation, visualization

When to Call Your Healthcare Provider

Contact your provider immediately if you experience:

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding
  • Severe or persistent headache that doesn't go away
  • Vision changes (blurring, seeing spots, light sensitivity)
  • Sudden severe swelling of face, hands, or feet
  • Decreased baby movement (fewer than 10 kicks in 2 hours)
  • Fluid leaking from vagina (possible water breaking)
  • Regular contractions before 37 weeks (possible preterm labor)
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Fever over 100.4°F (38°C)
  • Pain or burning during urination

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the third trimester

When does the third trimester start and end?

+

The third trimester begins at week 28 and continues until birth, typically around week 40. It covers weeks 28 through 42 (months 7-9), with full-term considered 37-42 weeks.

When should I pack my hospital bag?

+

Most experts recommend having your hospital bag packed by week 36. This ensures you're prepared if labor starts earlier than expected. Include essentials for you, your birth partner, and baby.

What are the signs of labor?

+

Key signs of labor include: regular contractions that get closer and stronger, water breaking (amniotic fluid leaking), bloody show (mucus plug discharge), lower back pain, and the urge to push. Call your provider when contractions are 5 minutes apart for 1 hour.

What is the difference between Braxton Hicks and real contractions?

+

Braxton Hicks are irregular practice contractions that don't get progressively stronger or closer together. Real labor contractions are regular, increase in intensity and frequency, and don't go away with rest, position changes, or hydration.

What is a birth plan?

+

A birth plan is a document outlining your preferences for labor and delivery. It can include preferences for pain management, positions for labor, who you want present, immediate postpartum care, feeding plans, and more. Discuss it with your healthcare provider.

When is a baby considered full term?

+

A baby is considered early term at 37 weeks, full term at 39-40 weeks, and late term at 41 weeks. Post-term is 42 weeks and beyond. Most providers recommend delivery by 42 weeks if labor hasn't started naturally.
