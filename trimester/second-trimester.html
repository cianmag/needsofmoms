Second Trimester

Second Trimester Weeks 13-27

Often called the "golden trimester," the second trimester brings renewed energy, your baby's first kicks, and the exciting anatomy scan where you can see your growing baby in detail.

← First Trimester Third Trimester →

Overview of the Second Trimester

The second trimester is when many moms start to truly enjoy pregnancy. Morning sickness typically fades, energy returns, and you'll begin to feel your baby move. Your belly grows noticeably, and you may find out if you're having a boy or girl at the anatomy scan.

During these 15 weeks, your baby grows from about the size of a peapod to nearly the size of a rutabaga. Major milestones include the development of fingerprints, the ability to hear sounds, and the first recognizable kicks and rolls.

Key Milestones

Week 13-15

Fingerprints form. Vocal cords develop. Facial expressions begin. Energy boost arrives!

Week 16-18

Eyes sensitive to light. Skeleton hardens. First movements felt (quickening). Anatomy scan around week 20.

Week 19-22

Vernix coating forms. Baby can hear sounds. Lanugo (fine hair) covers body. Organs maturing rapidly.

Week 23-27

Baby practices breathing. Eyes can open. Responds to sound and touch. Rapid weight gain begins.

Common Second Trimester Symptoms

While many first trimester symptoms ease, new ones may appear as your body continues to change:

  • Growing belly — Your uterus expands and you'll start to "show"
  • Backache — As your belly grows, your center of shift changes
  • Round ligament pain — Sharp or aching pain in the lower abdomen
  • Skin changes — Linea nigra (dark line on belly), stretch marks, melasma
  • Nasal congestion — Increased blood flow causes swelling in nasal passages
  • Leg cramps — Especially at night, often related to calcium or magnesium
  • Increased appetite — You may need about 340 extra calories per day
  • Braxton Hicks contractions — Practice contractions that are usually painless
  • Skin itching — As skin stretches over your growing belly

Tips & Advice for the Second Trimester

  1. Stay active — Walking, swimming, and prenatal exercise classes are excellent
  2. Practice good posture — Supports your back as your belly grows
  3. Sleep on your side — Left side is ideal for blood flow; use a pregnancy pillow
  4. Moisturize your skin — Helps with stretching and itching
  5. Start pelvic floor exercises — Kegels help prepare for labor and recovery
  6. Eat nutrient-dense foods — Focus on iron, calcium, and protein
  7. Announce your pregnancy — If you haven't already, this is a common time
  8. Begin baby planning — Research registries, start the nursery, consider classes
  9. Attend your anatomy scan — Usually weeks 18-22, a major milestone
  10. Manage varicose veins — Elevate legs, wear compression socks, stay active

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about the second trimester

When does the second trimester start and end?

+

The second trimester covers weeks 13 through 27 of pregnancy (months 4-6). It begins after week 12 and ends when the third trimester starts at week 28.

When will I feel my baby kick for the first time?

+

Most moms feel their baby's first movements (quickening) between weeks 16 and 25 of pregnancy. First-time moms often notice it around weeks 18-25, while experienced moms may feel it as early as week 13-16.

When is the anatomy scan?

+

The mid-pregnancy anatomy ultrasound (also called the 20-week scan) is typically performed between weeks 18 and 22. This detailed scan checks baby's organs, growth, and can reveal the sex if you want to know.

Is the second trimester the easiest?

+

Many women find the second trimester the most comfortable. Morning sickness often subsides, energy returns, and the belly isn't yet large enough to cause significant discomfort. This period is sometimes called the 'honeymoon phase' of pregnancy.

What tests happen during the second trimester?

+

Common second trimester tests include the anatomy ultrasound (18-22 weeks), glucose screening for gestational diabetes (24-28 weeks), and optional quad screen blood test (15-22 weeks). Your provider will recommend what's right for you.
