Pregnancy Symptoms Guide: What to Expect by Trimester

Pregnancy brings a remarkable array of changes to your body, and understanding these symptoms can help you feel more prepared and confident. This comprehensive guide covers the most common pregnancy symptoms organized by trimester, so you know what's normal and when to seek help.

First Trimester Symptoms (Weeks 1-12)

The first trimester is often when symptoms are most intense as your body undergoes rapid hormonal changes. Here are the most common symptoms you may experience:

Nausea and Morning Sickness

Up to 80% of pregnant women experience nausea, commonly called "morning sickness," though it can strike at any time. It typically begins around week 6, peaks around weeks 8-10, and usually resolves by week 14-16. To manage it, try eating small frequent meals, keeping crackers by your bedside, staying hydrated, and avoiding triggers like strong smells. Ginger tea and vitamin B6 supplements can also help.

Fatigue

Extreme tiredness is one of the earliest and most common symptoms. Your body is working incredibly hard to grow a new life, and rising progesterone levels contribute to drowsiness. Listen to your body — nap when possible, go to bed earlier, and don't hesitate to delegate tasks.

Food Aversions and Cravings

Many women develop strong aversions to certain foods, often those with strong odors. Simultaneously, unusual cravings can emerge. While it's fine to indulge cravings in moderation, aim for a balanced diet rich in folate, iron, calcium, and protein.

Breast Tenderness

Sore, swollen, and tender breasts are common as early as 1-2 weeks after conception. Hormonal changes increase blood flow and cause milk ducts to grow. A supportive bra can provide relief.

Frequent Urination

Increased blood volume and hormonal changes cause your kidneys to process more fluid. As your uterus grows, it also presses on your bladder. Stay hydrated but reduce fluids before bedtime.

Mood Swings

Fluctuating hormones can cause emotional ups and downs. You may feel tearful, irritable, or anxious one moment and elated the next. This is completely normal. Rest, gentle exercise, and talking to supportive friends or family can help.

Second Trimester Symptoms (Weeks 13-27)

Often called the "golden trimester," many first-trimester symptoms ease as your body adjusts. However, new symptoms may emerge:

Back Pain

As your baby grows and your center of gravity shifts, lower back pain becomes common. Your ligaments also loosen due to the hormone relaxin. Practice good posture, wear supportive shoes, use a pregnancy pillow, and consider prenatal massage or gentle stretching.

Round Ligament Pain

Sharp or aching pain on one or both sides of the lower abdomen is caused by the stretching of ligaments supporting your growing uterus. It often worsens with sudden movements. Move slowly when changing positions and rest when needed.

Skin Changes

You may notice a dark line (linea nigra) running down your abdomen, darkening of the areolas, and possible melasma (dark patches on the face). The "pregnancy glow" is real — increased blood flow gives skin a flushed, radiant appearance. Use sunscreen to protect against pigmentation changes.

Nasal Congestion and Nosebleeds

Increased blood volume and hormonal changes can cause swollen nasal passages and occasional nosebleeds. Use a humidifier and saline spray for relief.

Third Trimester Symptoms (Weeks 28-40)

The final stretch brings its own set of challenges as your body prepares for labor:

Swelling (Edema)

Swelling in the feet, ankles, and hands is very common in the third trimester due to increased fluid retention. Elevate your feet when possible, stay hydrated, and avoid standing for long periods. Sudden severe swelling in your face and hands could signal preeclampsia — contact your doctor immediately.

Braxton Hicks Contractions

These "practice contractions" cause your uterus to tighten irregularly. They're usually painless and irregular, unlike true labor contractions. Stay hydrated and change positions to ease them.

Shortness of Breath

Your growing uterus pushes against your diaphragm, making it harder to take deep breaths. Practice good posture and sleep slightly propped up on pillows.

Heartburn and Indigestion

Progesterone relaxes the valve between your stomach and esophagus, allowing acid to reflux. Eat smaller meals, avoid spicy or fatty foods, and don't lie down right after eating.

Difficulty Sleeping

Frequent bathroom trips, discomfort, and anxiety can disrupt sleep. Try sleeping on your left side with pillows between your knees and under your belly. A consistent bedtime routine can help.

Pelvic Pressure

As your baby drops lower into your pelvis (lightening), you may feel increased pressure and heaviness. This is normal as your body prepares for delivery.

When to Call Your Doctor

While most pregnancy symptoms are normal, some require immediate medical attention. Contact your healthcare provider right away if you experience:

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding or passing clots
  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain
  • Severe headaches with vision changes (blurry vision, seeing spots)
  • High fever (over 101°F / 38.3°C)
  • Pain or burning during urination
  • Sudden severe swelling in face, hands, or feet
  • Thoughts of self-harm or harming your baby
  • Decreased fetal movement (after 28 weeks, do kick counts daily)
  • Fluid leaking from vagina before 37 weeks
  • Regular painful contractions before 37 weeks

Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, it's always better to call and be reassured. Your healthcare team is there to support you. For more details on what to expect throughout your pregnancy, see our What to Expect During Pregnancy guide.
