How is the pregnancy due date calculated?

The standard method for calculating an estimated due date (EDD) is Naegele's rule. This rule adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last normal menstrual period (LMP). It assumes a regular 28-day cycle where ovulation happens around day 14. This calculates your gestational age.

If you know your exact conception date (e.g., through IVF or precise ovulation tracking), the calculation simply adds 266 days to that specific date, providing a potentially more accurate estimate.

radiology

Accuracy of Estimates

While calculators provide a great baseline, an early ultrasound (dating scan) performed in the first trimester is generally considered the most accurate method for confirming your due date and gestational age by measuring the fetal crown-rump length.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is gestational age?

Gestational age is the common term used during pregnancy to describe how far along the pregnancy is. It is measured in weeks, from the first day of the woman's last menstrual cycle to the current date.

Can my due date change?

Yes, it's very common for due dates to be adjusted. Your healthcare provider may change your estimated due date based on ultrasound measurements, especially if it's performed in the first trimester.
