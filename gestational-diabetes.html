Gestational Diabetes: Causes, Diet, and Management

Understanding gestational diabetes — what it is, risk factors, how it's diagnosed, diet management, and what it means for you and your baby.

Gestational diabetes affects about 6-9% of pregnancies. While the diagnosis can be concerning, with proper management, most women with gestational diabetes have healthy pregnancies and healthy babies.

What Is Gestational Diabetes?

Gestational diabetes is a type of diabetes that develops during pregnancy in women who didn't have diabetes before. It occurs when your body can't produce enough insulin to overcome the insulin resistance caused by pregnancy hormones, leading to high blood sugar levels.

Risk Factors

You're at higher risk for gestational diabetes if you:

  • Are overweight or obese (BMI over 25)
  • Are over 25 years old
  • Have a family history of diabetes
  • Had gestational diabetes in a previous pregnancy
  • Have previously delivered a baby weighing 9+ pounds
  • Have polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
  • Are of certain ethnic backgrounds (Hispanic, African American, Native American, South Asian, East Asian)

How Is It Diagnosed?

Most women are screened for gestational diabetes between 24-28 weeks of pregnancy. The screening involves:

Glucose Challenge Test (GCT)

You'll drink a sweet glucose solution and have your blood drawn one hour later. If your blood sugar is above 140 mg/dL, you'll need the follow-up test.

Glucose Tolerance Test (GTT)

This 3-hour fasting test confirms the diagnosis. You'll drink a glucose solution and have your blood drawn at 1, 2, and 3 hours. Two or more abnormal readings indicate gestational diabetes.

Gestational Diabetes Diet

Diet is the primary treatment for gestational diabetes. The goal is to keep your blood sugar stable throughout the day.

Key Principles

  • Eat 3 small meals and 2-3 snacks daily — don't skip meals
  • Limit carbohydrates — focus on complex carbs over simple sugars
  • Pair carbs with protein — helps slow sugar absorption
  • Eat plenty of fiber — vegetables, whole grains, legumes
  • Avoid sugary drinks — water, unsweetened tea, and milk are best
  • Watch portion sizes — use measuring cups initially

Foods to Focus On

  • Lean proteins (chicken, fish, eggs, tofu)
  • Non-starchy vegetables (broccoli, spinach, peppers, tomatoes)
  • Whole grains (brown rice, quinoa, whole wheat bread)
  • Healthy fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil)
  • Low-glycemic fruits (berries, apples, pears)

Foods to Limit or Avoid

  • Sugary drinks (soda, juice, sweetened coffee drinks)
  • White bread, white rice, regular pasta
  • Sweets, candy, cookies, cake
  • Fruit juice (even 100% juice)
  • Processed snacks (chips, crackers)

Monitoring Blood Sugar

You'll likely need to check your blood sugar 4 times daily:

  • Fasting (first thing in the morning)
  • 1 hour after breakfast
  • 1 hour after lunch
  • 1 hour after dinner

Target ranges: Fasting under 95 mg/dL, 1-hour post-meal under 140 mg/dL.

What It Means for Your Baby

With proper management, most babies of mothers with gestational diabetes are healthy. Unmanaged gestational diabetes can lead to:

  • Excessive birth weight (macrosomia)
  • Preterm birth
  • Low blood sugar after birth
  • Higher risk of obesity and type 2 diabetes later in life
