Am I Pregnant? 10 Early Signs of Pregnancy

Wondering if you might be pregnant? Learn the 10 earliest signs of pregnancy, when to take a test, and what steps to take next.

Wondering whether those subtle changes in your body might mean you're pregnant? Early pregnancy symptoms can be easy to miss, especially if you're not actively trying to conceive. While a pregnancy test is the only definitive way to confirm pregnancy, there are several early signs that might indicate you're expecting.

10 Early Signs of Pregnancy

💡 Tip: Every woman's experience is different. You may have all of these symptoms, just a few, or none at all. Not having symptoms doesn't mean you're not pregnant!

1. Missed Period

The most obvious and reliable sign of pregnancy is a missed period. If you have a regular menstrual cycle and your period is late by a week or more, it's time to take a pregnancy test. However, missed periods can also be caused by stress, weight changes, or hormonal imbalances.

2. Tender, Swollen Breasts

Hormonal changes in early pregnancy can make your breasts sore, tender, and swollen. This symptom can start as early as one to two weeks after conception. You may notice your bra feels tighter than usual or that your breasts are more sensitive to touch.

3. Nausea With or Without Vomiting

Morning sickness can begin as early as two weeks after conception. Despite its name, nausea during pregnancy can strike at any time of day. Some women experience mild queasiness while others have severe vomiting. Check our morning sickness guide for remedies.

4. Increased Urination

You might find yourself running to the bathroom more often than usual. During pregnancy, your blood volume increases, which causes your kidneys to process more fluid that ends up in your bladder.

5. Fatigue

Feeling unusually tired? Fatigue is one of the most common early pregnancy symptoms. Rising progesterone levels can make you feel sleepy, and lower blood sugar levels and increased blood production can drain your energy.

6. Food Aversions or Cravings

You might suddenly find certain foods repulsive or develop cravings for foods you never cared for before. These changes in food preferences can be triggered by hormonal changes and may last throughout your pregnancy.

7. Bloating and Constipation

Hormonal changes can slow down your digestive system, leading to bloating and constipation. These symptoms are similar to what many women experience before their period, which is why they're often overlooked as early pregnancy signs.

8. Light Spotting and Cramping

Some women experience light spotting known as implantation bleeding when the fertilized egg attaches to the uterine lining. This typically occurs 10-14 days after conception and is lighter and shorter than a regular period.

9. Mood Swings

The flood of hormones in early pregnancy can make you unusually emotional and weepy. Mood swings are common and can range from joy and excitement to anxiety and irritability.

10. Elevated Basal Body Temperature

If you've been tracking your basal body temperature and notice it stays elevated for 18 or more days after ovulation, you're likely pregnant.

When to Take a Pregnancy Test

Home pregnancy tests are most accurate when taken after your missed period. Most tests can detect the pregnancy hormone hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) in your urine. For the best results:

  • Wait until at least the first day of your missed period
  • Use your first morning urine (most concentrated)
  • Follow the test instructions carefully
  • If negative but symptoms persist, wait a few days and test again

What to Do If You're Pregnant

If your test is positive, congratulations! 🎉 Here are your next steps:

  • Schedule a prenatal appointment — Most providers want to see you at 8 weeks
  • Start taking prenatal vitamins — Especially folic acid (400-800 mcg daily)
  • Calculate your due date — Use our Due Date Calculator
  • Learn about your baby's development — Browse our Week by Week Guide
  • Avoid harmful substances — Stop alcohol, smoking, and certain medications
  • Eat well and rest — Check our pregnancy diet guide
